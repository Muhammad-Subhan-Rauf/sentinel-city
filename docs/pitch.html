The problem & the AI solution

Disaster response is bottlenecked by human coordination.

The problem

Thousands of concurrent, duplicate, sometimes fake 911 reports must be fused into one incident, sized, cordoned, matched to the nearest units, and turned into a public warning — today through overloaded humans on radios, one incident at a time. The citizen inside the zone gets no guidance at all.

The AI solution

An event-driven AI pipeline puts an agent on that loop: it triages reports, triangulates them into one incident, then autonomously declares · cordons · dispatches · warns — operator-supervised, and every action stamped source='ai' and replayable.

Why now — 2024 saw 27 separate US billion-dollar weather/climate disasters totaling $182.7B (4th-costliest on record · NOAA/climate.gov 2024); DHS shipped a Generative-AI emergency-response playbook (Jan 2025).

Business model & competitive edge

The moat isn't the model — it's auditable, supervised AI.

$

Revenue — per-city SaaS

Annual license to emergency-mgmt agencies & 911 centers, tiered by population + incident volume, plus usage-based AI inference. Land with a paid pilot, expand across stations & neighbors.

The moat — gov-grade trust

Every AI action stamped source='ai', row-locked, fully replayable; deterministic rails constrain the model. The auditability & supervision government procurement requires — a generic chatbot can't clear that bar.

⊕

vs. status quo

We don't replace legacy CAD (Motorola, Tyler) — we're the agentic layer on top of it, proposing actions a supervised operator confirms. Not a rip-and-replace.
